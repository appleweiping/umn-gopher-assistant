# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

UMN Gopher Assistant welcomes contributors and users of different backgrounds,
identities, abilities, languages, disciplines, and experience levels. We commit
to a collaborative environment where people can participate without
harassment, intimidation, discrimination, or retaliation.

This is an independent, unofficial project. Participation does not represent
the University of Minnesota, and no participant may use the project to imply
University endorsement or authority.

## Expected behavior

Participants should:

- communicate with respect and assume good intent while still addressing
  concrete harm;
- give specific, actionable feedback about work rather than attacking a person;
- make room for questions, language differences, and different levels of
  technical experience;
- credit ideas, code, translations, research, and assets accurately;
- disclose relevant conflicts of interest;
- respect privacy, consent, accessibility needs, and personal boundaries;
- accept moderation decisions and use the appeal path instead of escalating a
  conflict publicly;
- protect confidential security, conduct, and personal information;
- correct misleading claims about official, verified, or schematic content.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following are not acceptable:

- harassment, threats, stalking, intimidation, or unwanted sexual attention;
- discriminatory or demeaning language, imagery, jokes, or exclusion;
- deliberate misgendering, deadnaming, or disclosure of another person's
  identity or private information;
- sustained disruption, trolling, personal attacks, or bad-faith argument;
- retaliation against someone who reports a concern or participates in review;
- impersonating a person, maintainer, University representative, or official
  source;
- publishing private messages, security details, credentials, location data,
  student records, or other sensitive information without permission;
- contributing unlicensed assets or data and concealing their origin;
- pressuring someone to test with real University accounts or protected
  systems;
- using community or AI features to harass, deceive, discriminate, or create
  unsafe guidance.

Good-faith disagreement about technical or policy choices is welcome when it
remains focused, evidence-based, and respectful.

## Scope

This Code applies in project-controlled spaces and when a person is
representing the project, including:

- repositories, issues, reviews, discussions, chats, and meetings;
- project demos, events, and community spaces;
- direct messages used for project work;
- public statements made on behalf of the project.

It does not grant project maintainers authority over unrelated private conduct,
but conduct outside project spaces may be considered when it creates a credible
safety risk to participants or the project.

## Reporting

Use the repository host's private moderation or maintainer-contact channel when
available. If no private channel is configured, open a public issue requesting
private contact and include no names, screenshots, messages, or sensitive
details.

A report may include:

- what happened and where;
- links or timestamps that help preserve context;
- immediate safety or privacy concerns;
- whether the behavior is continuing;
- the response requested;
- a safe way to follow up.

Do not investigate by accessing another person's account or private data.
Reports about a software vulnerability belong in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md).
If there is immediate danger, contact the appropriate local emergency service;
this project is not an emergency response channel.

## Handling reports

Maintainers responsible for a report should:

- disclose conflicts and recuse themselves when appropriate;
- limit report access to people needed for review;
- avoid promising confidentiality they cannot technically guarantee;
- seek enough context for a fair decision without demanding unnecessary
  personal information;
- prioritize participant safety and prevent retaliation;
- document the decision and retention need securely;
- tell the reporter what can be shared about the outcome.

Reports made in bad faith to intimidate, expose, or retaliate against another
person can themselves violate this Code. A report is not bad faith merely
because evidence is incomplete or a concern cannot be substantiated.

## Enforcement

Responses should be proportionate to severity, pattern, intent, and impact.
They may include:

1. a private clarification or request to correct behavior;
2. a warning and temporary limits on participation;
3. removal of content or privileges;
4. temporary suspension;
5. permanent removal from project-controlled spaces;
6. escalation to the relevant hosting provider or lawful authority when
   necessary for safety or legal compliance.

Maintainers may take immediate temporary action while reviewing a credible
threat, privacy exposure, credential leak, or ongoing harassment.

## Appeals

A person affected by a moderation decision may request review through a private
maintainer channel. The appeal should identify the decision, relevant missing
context, and desired remedy. When possible, a maintainer who did not make the
original decision should review the appeal.

## Maintainer accountability

Maintainers are also subject to this Code. Project status, technical expertise,
institutional affiliation, or volume of contributions does not exempt anyone
from respectful behavior or conflict-of-interest review.
